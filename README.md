## 📌 Overview  
This project explores classical diffusion models (DDPMs) and proposes a modified forward process where **Gaussian noise is replaced with deterministic chaos** generated by the **logistic map**. The goal was to investigate whether **chaotic yet deterministic signals** can substitute traditional stochastic noise while retaining generative quality and improving compute efficiency.

---

## 🧠 Classical Diffusion Models (DDPM)

Diffusion models transform a clean image \(x_0\) into noise through a forward process:

\[
x_t = \sqrt{\alpha_t}\,x_{t-1} + \sqrt{1-\alpha_t}\,\epsilon,\quad \epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0, I)
\]

This leads to a closed-form:

\[
x_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\,\epsilon
\]

The training objective is noise prediction:

\[
\mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E} \|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)\|^2
\]

The reverse Markov chain denoises step-by-step until we reconstruct \(x_0\).

---

## 🔄 Sampling  
Start with pure Gaussian noise:

\[
x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)
\]

Iteratively denoise:

\[
x_{t-1} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}\left(x_t - 
\frac{1 - \alpha_t}{\sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}}\,\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)\right)
+ \sigma_t z
\]

This yields realistic image samples.

---

## 🌀 Logistic-Map-Based Noising  
Instead of Gaussian noise, the project explores deterministic chaos using the **logistic map at \(r=4\)**:

\[
x_{t+1} = 4x_t(1 - x_t)
\]

For almost any \(x_0\in(0,1)\), the sequence becomes chaotic and follows:

\[
x_t \sim \text{Beta}(0.5,0.5)
\]

### Constructing the Logistic Noise  
We define the noisy image:

\[
i_t = 4 i_{t-1}(1 - i_{t-1})
\]

Total noise:

\[
\mathcal{N}(i) = i_t - i_0
\]

This replaces Gaussian randomness with deterministic chaos.

---

## 🎯 Logistic-Based Training Objective  

Instead of noise prediction, we try to **directly recover** \(i_0\):

\[
\mathcal{L} = \|i_0 - i'(x_t, t)\|^2
\]

### Motivation  
Hypothesis:  
> Deterministic chaos is easier to learn → faster / more stable training vs. stochastic diffusion.

## 🧪 Experiments & Results

### 🖼 Dataset: MNIST  
#### CNN & U-Net  
- Both failed to learn meaningful reconstruction.  
- Inputs were fully noisy → CNN-based architectures could not extract features.

#### ✅ Feed Forward Network (FFN)  
- Worked surprisingly well **when trained on a single label (e.g., digit 7)**.  
- However, outputs lacked **style diversity**.  
- Model converged to a **central average image**.

#### Random Forest  
- Similar behavior to FFN.  
- Some pixel-level variation but **no handwriting-style diversity**.

#### ❌ RNN  
- Failed due to vanishing gradients.  
- Logistic noise compresses most values near 0 and 1 → unstable gradients.

#### 🔄 VAE  
- Introducing randomness in latent space produced **diversity in handwriting**.  
- Logistic noise alone is insufficient — *stochasticity is still needed*.

---

## 🧪 CIFAR-10 Experiments  
- VAE + logistic noise shows slight structure learning.  
- CNN-decoder VAEs performed better but still poor quality.  
- Logistic chaos alone is too restrictive for complex datasets.

---

## 🔍 Key Insight  
Chaotic noise **does not naturally provide variation** needed for generative diversity.  
It collapses to central templates.

Adding even small randomness (e.g., VAE latent sampling) dramatically improves output quality.

**Conclusion:**  
> Logistic noising can replace Gaussian noise **only as a partial component**.  
> Pure deterministic chaos fails to yield realistic generative diversity.

## 🚀 Future Work  
### 1. Logistic Noise in VAE Latent Space  
Use logistic chaotic updates in latent space instead of pixel space.

### 2. Exploit Logistic Map Invertibility  
The inverse logistic map:

\[
x_{t-1} = \frac{-\theta \pm \sqrt{\theta^2 - 4\theta x_t}}{2\theta}
\]

This leads to a **binary tree with \(2^t\) possible paths**.

Idea:  
Train a **Transformer** to predict the correct reverse path.

### 3. Hybrid Chaotic–Random Diffusion  
Combine low-cost chaos + small stochastic term.

---

## 🧾 Conclusion  
This project compared classical Gaussian diffusion with logistic-map-based deterministic chaos.  
Findings:

- Logistic chaos **does not naturally generate diversity**.  
- FFNs and Random Forests can partially learn structure but fail at stylistic variation.  
- CNNs/U-Nets do not work on fully chaotic images.  
- Adding randomness (e.g., VAE) regains generative ability.  
- Logistic noise may still be useful for **efficient noising**, especially in latent space.
